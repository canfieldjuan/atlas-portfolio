# Body Copy — Section 2: The Current Way vs. This Way

---

You have already tried to fix this.

You hired more agents. But at 30% to 45% annual turnover and $10,000 to
$20,000 per replacement, linear hiring is a leaky bucket. The math does not
close.

You built out the knowledge base. It degraded within six months. Content went
stale. Search stayed poor. Gartner found that 43% of self-service failures
happen because customers cannot find content relevant to their issue — even
when it exists. Nobody owned the feedback loop from ticket to article gap.

You deployed a chatbot. Klarna's CEO said publicly in May 2025 what a lot of
teams discovered quietly: "As cost unfortunately seems to have been a too
predominant evaluation factor when organizing this, what you end up having is
lower quality." Intercom Fin users report real-world resolution rates far
below vendor case studies — often because conversations get marked "assumed
resolved" the moment a human steps in.

You wrote macros. They went outdated as the product changed. Became the reason
the bot sent the wrong article. Gorgias's own product copy admits: "Generic,
robotic responses frustrate customers."

You may have tried outsourcing. First-year attrition runs higher offshore.
Quality drops on complex tickets. Klarna tried BPO, pivoted to AI, and then
walked AI back — the full loop — because none of it addressed the root cause.

None of these failed because you did them wrong. They failed because none of
them close the loop between what a customer asks and what your help center
says. The answer gets written — in a ticket reply, in a macro, in a KB article
nobody maintains — and then it disappears back into the queue.

---

There is a different way to approach this.

| The current way | Support Ticket Deflection |
|---|---|
| Scan tags. Poll agents. Chase the loudest complaint. | Upload a CSV. Within 24 hours, receive a ranked fix list — ordered by how often each question reaches support, backed by the actual tickets and verbatim customer quotes that produced each finding. |
| Write docs from internal assumptions — the language your team uses internally, not the language customers type into Google. | Write docs from customer language extracted from real tickets — the exact words people already search, so Google can match them. |
| Guess which fixes matter most. The squeaky-wheel customer. The complaint that reached the CEO's inbox. The macro someone remembers writing. | Frequency-ranked, not opinion-ranked. The queue sets the priority. The data decides. |
| Hope customers find the answer. Publish the doc. Wait. | Customer language matches Google searches. Google indexes the page. Google deflects the ticket before the customer ever reaches your support form. |
| No feedback loop. The answer disappears into the ticket history. Next week, someone asks again. | Every recommendation links to its source tickets. Click through. Read the original customer language. Verify the finding yourself. |
| "The bot just sent the wrong article." Macros go stale. AI marks conversations "assumed resolved." | You review every recommendation. You approve every FAQ. Nothing goes live without you. |
| Hire more agents to answer the same questions faster. | Remove the questions from the queue. Let your existing agents do the work they were hired to do. |

---

The mechanism is extraction, not automation. Support Ticket Deflection does not
answer tickets. It does not touch your help desk. It does not publish anything
without your review. It reads your ticket history, finds the pattern, and hands
you a prioritized fix list with drafted answers. You decide what happens next.
