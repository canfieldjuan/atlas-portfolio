# Body Copy Outline — Support Ticket Deflection Landing Page

Each section maps to a landing-page zone. The outline specifies the persuasive
job, the data drawn from `body-copy-data-brief.md`, and the Ogilvy tactic used.

---

## SECTION 1 — Problem Agitation

**Landing-page zone:** Below the hero. "Here's what's broken."

**Persuasive job:** Make the reader feel the cost of staying with the current
way. Open with the hard cost (CFO-visible), then layer in the human cost
(agent burnout), then the customer cost (churn). Close with the blindspot —
they know it's bad, but they don't know how bad.

**Structure (5 paragraphs):**

| Para | Topic | Key data | Tactic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The cost gap | Gartner: $1.84 vs $13.50 (7.3x). HDI: $22.00 vs $2.93. 80–100x across full journey. | Lead with the number. Ogilvy: "The consumer isn't a moron, she's your wife." Respect the reader's intelligence with hard data. |
| 2 | The repeat loop | 40–60% of volume is repetitive. Leaders estimate up to 40% of live volume could've been self-served. The 6-step guesswork workflow. The 5-step repeat loop. | Name the loop they're stuck in. Schwartz: "The reader already has this problem. You're just articulating it." |
| 3 | The agent cost | Salesforce: 39% of time servicing customers. 5 hrs/week lost. "Mind-numbing repetition" (NICE). 90 seconds typing / 10 minutes context-switching. | Move from abstract cost → human experience. Make it visceral. |
| 4 | The burnout chain → attrition | 31–45% turnover. $10K–$20K/replacement. 74% burnout. 90% say repetition kills morale. | Connect the dots: repetition → burnout → turnover → cost. |
| 5 | The customer cost + blindspot | Gartner: 14% self-service resolution. 94% vs 4% repurchase intent (low vs high effort). Leaders estimate 60% self-service demand, actual is 81%. 28-point proactivity perception gap. | Close the loop. Problem is bigger than they think. |

**Page placement:** 3–5 scrolls below hero. This is the section that earns the
right to pitch the mechanism.

## SECTION 2 — The Current Way vs. This Way

**Landing-page zone:** The pivot. After the problem, before the mechanism.

**Persuasive job:** Acknowledge what they've tried. Show why it underperformed.
This earns trust before the pitch.

**Structure (1 paragraph of acknowledgment + 7 short comparison pairs):**

**Opening paragraph:**
"You've already tried..." — name the 6 things (hiring, KB, chatbots, macros,
BPO, guessing at priorities, thinking the answers already exist). Validate
the effort. Then pivot: "None of these failed because you did them wrong.
They failed because they don't close the loop between what customers ask and
what your help center says."

**Comparison table or alternating pairs:**

| Current way | This way |
|---|---|
| Scan tags, poll agents, chase complaints | CSV upload → ranked fix list in 24 hours |
| Write docs from internal assumptions | Write docs from customer language extracted from real tickets |
| Guess which fixes matter most | Frequency-ranked, not opinion-ranked |
| Hope customers find it | Customer language matches Google searches → Google deflects |
| No feedback loop | Every recommendation links to source tickets |
| "The bot just sent the wrong article" | You review and publish. Nothing goes live without you. |
| Hire more agents to answer the same questions faster | Remove the questions from the queue |

**Page placement:** The transition zone. Bridges problem → mechanism.

## SECTION 3 — The Mechanism

**Landing-page zone:** "How it works." The core pitch.

**Persuasive job:** Make the mechanism clear enough that the reader can explain
it to their boss in 30 seconds. Three steps, no jargon.

**Structure (3-step numbered flow):**

```
Step 1: Upload a CSV of your past support tickets.
        → Takes under 5 minutes. Export from Zendesk, Intercom, Help Scout,
          or any help desk that gives you a CSV.

Step 2: In 24 hours, you receive a ranked fix list.
        → What's in it:
          • The questions customers keep asking — in their own words, ranked
            by how often they reach support.
          • The customer-term-to-doc-term gaps — every mismatch keeping people
            from finding answers you've already written.
          • The source tickets and quotes behind each recommendation.
          • A drafted FAQ for each gap, built from real ticket evidence.

Step 3: You review, edit, publish what you approve.
        → Nothing goes live without you. Pages go up in customer language.
          Google indexes them. The next customer who searches the same
          question finds your page, not your support form.
```

**The SEO multiplier paragraph (after the 3 steps):**
Why this works beyond the help center: pages written in the language people
search get more visibility on Google. Your ticket queue already contains that
language. Customers type "how do I cancel." Your help center says "Subscription
Termination." Google can't match them. Close that gap, and Google starts
deflecting tickets before the customer ever reaches your support form.

**What it does NOT do (sidebar or callout):**
- Does not answer tickets.
- Does not touch your help desk.
- Does not publish anything without your review.
- Does not replace agents.

**Page placement:** Below the pivot section. This is the "how" the reader has
been waiting for.

## SECTION 4 — Proof Stack

**Landing-page zone:** "Why you should believe this."

**Persuasive job:** Stack product-level proof (strongest), then borrowed proof
(industry data), then third-party authority (analyst quotes). End with the
Klarna cautionary tale as honesty signal.

**Structure (4 blocks):**

**Block 1: Product-level proof (3 bullets)**
What we can prove from the product itself — no external source needed.
- Source-ticket traceability: every recommendation links to real tickets.
- Frequency-ranked, not opinion-ranked: the queue sets the priority.
- Drafted from evidence, not invented: FAQs built from real agent responses.

**Block 2: Borrowed proof (4 bullets)**
Industry data that validates the problem we solve.
- Gartner: 7.3x cost gap ($1.84 self-service vs $13.50 assisted) **[M]**
- Gartner: only 14% of issues fully resolve in self-service **[M]**
- SQM: 1% FCR improvement = 1% operating cost reduction **[M]**
- CEB: 94% repurchase for low-effort vs 4% for high-effort **[M]**

**Block 3: Analyst authority (2–3 pull-quotes)**
Third-party voices that make the reader nod.
- Eric Keller, Gartner: "While 73% of customers use self-service... it's
  concerning to see that so few fully resolve there."
- Nick Toman, The Effortless Experience: "We count that as yet another
  'first contact resolved,' but the customer views it as 'I had to contact
  you twice.'"

**Block 4: The Klarna lesson (paragraph)**
February 2024: AI handled 2.3M conversations. May 2025: CEO walked it back.
Lesson: deflection without quality is a different cost line. Support Ticket
Deflection is built on this — you stay in control.

**Page placement:** Below the mechanism. The reader understands how it works.
Now they need to believe it's real.

## SECTION 5 — The Offer

**Landing-page zone:** "What you do and what you get."

**Persuasive job:** Make the exchange clear. This is the least persuasive
section — it's informational. The persuasion already happened. Just be clear.

**Structure (4 short blocks):**

**What you do:** Upload a CSV of past support tickets.

**What you get (within 24 hours):** Ranked fix list with 4 deliverables.
- Repeat question clusters with frequency ranking
- Customer-term-to-doc-term gaps
- Source tickets and verbatim customer quotes
- Drafted FAQs, ready to review/edit/publish

**What happens next:** You review → you publish → Google deflects.

**Who it's for / not for:**
- For: Support leads at 15–75-person B2B SaaS, exportable help desk, control
  their help center.
- Not for: Enterprise procurement, no ticket history, looking for a chatbot.

**Page placement:** Below proof. This is the "what" — the mechanism already
covered the "how."

## SECTION 6 — Risk Reversal

**Landing-page zone:** "Why this is safe to try."

**Persuasive job:** Remove the final objection before the CTA. The reader is
convinced it works. Now they need to feel safe taking the step.

**Structure (3 guarantees + risk list):**

**The control guarantee:** Nothing goes live without you. You review every
recommendation. You approve every FAQ. The system does not publish, does not
modify your help center, does not answer tickets.

**The hallucination guarantee:** Every recommendation links back to a real
ticket with a real customer quote. If it's in the report, it's in your queue.
Click through and verify.

**The timeline:** 24 hours from CSV to fix list. No consultant. No rollout.

**What you're not risking:** No AI talking to customers. No automated
publishing. No per-resolution pricing. No "assumed resolved" conversations.

**Page placement:** Immediately before the CTA. This is the last thing the
reader sees before deciding.

## TONE RULES (Ogilvy, applied to body copy)

1. Lead with facts — not adjectives, not hype.
2. Every paragraph should earn its place. If it doesn't move the argument
   forward, cut it.
3. Prefer concrete numbers and nouns over claims.
4. The reader is intelligent. They've already tried several things. Respect
   that.
5. No "transform," "streamline," "unlock," "supercharge," or "revolutionize."
6. The urgency comes from the cost of staying — not from fake scarcity.
7. Every claim must be attributable. If we can't source it, we don't say it.
8. Acknowledge what they've tried before pitching what's different.
