# Body Copy — Section 4: Proof Stack

---

**Every recommendation links to a real ticket.**

Not a model's best guess. Not synthesized from training data. For every finding
in your ranked fix list, you can click through to the source — the exact
ticket, the verbatim customer language, the precise moment the gap occurred.

If the report says customers are asking about exports, you can read the tickets
where they asked. If it flags a term gap between "cancel my account" and
"Subscription Termination," you can see the search that failed and the doc that
should have answered it.

Nothing is generated from thin air. You can verify every claim yourself.

---

**The queue sets the priority — not opinions.**

The fix list is ordered by how often each question reaches support. Not by
which agent complained loudest. Not by which topic the team assumes is the
biggest problem. Not by the complaint that reached the CEO's inbox.

The data decides. And because frequency correlates with search demand, the
highest-ranked fixes are also the highest-impact SEO opportunities.

---

**FAQs are drafted from real answers that already worked.**

Every draft FAQ is assembled from the actual responses your support team wrote
across closed tickets — in the language that already resolved the issue for a
real customer.

The troubleshooting steps. The resolution path. The phrasing that got the
customer to reply "thanks, that fixed it." All of it comes from answers your
team already proved. You are not inventing. You are publishing what already
worked.

---

**The industry data backs the problem — and the mechanism.**

Gartner benchmarked the cost gap: $1.84 for a self-service resolution versus
$13.50 for an assisted contact. A 7.3x multiplier on every ticket that should
never have reached an agent.

Gartner measured the resolution rate: 73% of customers attempt self-service.
Only 14% fully resolve there. Eric Keller, Senior Director at Gartner: "While
73% of customers use self-service at some point in their customer service
journey, it's concerning to see that so few fully resolve there."

SQM Group, whose First Contact Resolution benchmarks cover more than 500 North
American call centers, found that a 1% improvement in FCR reduces operating
costs by 1% — while increasing customer satisfaction by the same amount.

CEB research across 97,000 customers, published as *The Effortless Experience*,
found that 94% of customers with low-effort interactions intend to repurchase.
Only 4% of high-effort customers report the same intent. 81% of high-effort
customers intend to spread negative word of mouth.

Nick Toman, co-author of the study: "We count that as yet another 'first
contact resolved,' but the customer views it as 'I had to contact you twice.'"

---

**The Klarna lesson: deflection without quality is a different cost line.**

In February 2024, Klarna's AI assistant handled 2.3 million customer service
conversations in a single month — two-thirds of all chats. The company reported
it was doing "the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents" and projected $40
million in profit improvement for the year. Resolution time dropped from 11
minutes to under 2 minutes. Repeat inquiries fell 25%.

By May 2025, the CEO walked it back publicly. Sebastian Siemiatkowski told
Bloomberg: "As cost unfortunately seems to have been a too predominant
evaluation factor when organizing this, what you end up having is lower
quality. Really investing in the quality of the human support is the way of
the future for us."

The lesson is not that automation does not work. It is that automation without
human review produces automated mistakes. Deflection without quality is a
different cost line — you trade ticket volume for customer trust.

Support Ticket Deflection is built on that lesson. The system extracts the
pattern. It drafts the answers. It ranks the gaps. But nothing goes live
without you. You review every recommendation. You approve every word. You
remain the final author of everything that ships.
