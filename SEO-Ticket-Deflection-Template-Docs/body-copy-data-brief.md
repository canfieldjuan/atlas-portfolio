## SECTION 1 — Problem Agitation (make the pain real and measured)

### The cost gap (open with this — CFO-visible, sourced, undeniable)

- Gartner 2024: self-service costs $1.84/contact. Assisted costs $13.50.
  That's a 7.3x multiplier. Source: Gartner, "Benchmarks to Assess Your
  Customer Service Costs," J.J. Moncus, February 2024. **[M]**
- HDI 2024: $22.00 live agent vs $2.93 self-service. Source: HDI 2024 Support
  Center Practices report. **[M]**
- Across the full resolution journey with channel-switching: 80–100x.
  Source: Gartner / Devin Poole, 2019 (8,000+ customer journeys). **[M]**

### The repeat-contact multiplier (the hidden denominator)

- 30% of all interactions are repeat contacts on the same issue. Average issue
  requires 2.3 contacts. Source: Lorikeet 2026 synthesis. **[E]**
- 40–60% of inbound volume is repetitive queries. 70% in B2C. Source:
  Industry synthesis. **[E]**
- Gartner: 74% of issues reaching live agents could've been resolved via
  self-service if the article existed. Source: Gartner / CoSupport AI. **[M]**
- Each additional call drops CSAT 16%. Source: SQM Group. **[M]**
- 1% FCR improvement = 1% operating cost reduction. Source: SQM Group. **[M]**

### The agent-side cost (the human argument)

- Agents spend 39% of time servicing customers. Source: Salesforce State of
  Service 6th Edition, April 2024 (n=5,500+). **[M]**
- 90 seconds typing a macro reply. 10 minutes of context assembly (toggling
  between CRM, billing, ticketing tools). Source: Friction Multiplier
  synthesis. **[E]**
- 53% of agents at underperforming orgs toggle between multiple screens.
  Source: Salesforce State of Service 6th Edition. **[M]**
- 5 hours/week lost to repetitive tickets per agent. Source: Gorgias. **[M]**
- IT desk: 30–40% of hours on password resets and basic troubleshooting.
  Source: Flairstech. **[M]**

### The burnout-to-attrition chain (the structural cost)

- Annual turnover: 31.2% (2024). Source: Metrigy. **[M]**
- 40–45% industry average. 55–61% high-stress sectors. Source: Insignia. **[M]**
- Replacement cost: $10,000–$20,000/agent direct. Up to $46,000 total impact.
  Source: Metrigy / Insignia. **[M/E]**
- 90% of IT support pros: repetitive tasks → low morale. 74% ongoing burnout.
  Source: Insignia. **[M]**
- SQM: 38% turnover. 47% of managers cite it as #1 operating problem. **[M]**

### The customer-side cost (the churn argument)

- 73% attempt self-service. Only 14% fully resolve there. Source: Gartner,
  August 2024 (n=5,728). **[M]**
- 43% of failures: can't find relevant content. 45%: "company didn't
  understand what they were trying to do." Source: Same. **[M]**
- 94% of low-effort customers intend to repurchase. 4% of high-effort.
  Source: CEB / Effortless Experience (97,000+ customers). **[M]**
- 74% frustrated when they have to repeat information. Source: Zendesk CX
  Trends 2026 (n=11,000+). **[M]**
- 32% stop doing business after one negative experience. Source: PwC. **[M]**

### The leadership blindspot (they don't know how bad it is)

  Devin Poole, 2019. **[M]**
- 81% of consumers want more self-service. Leaders estimate ~60%.

- 61% of teams believe they're proactive. 33% of customers agree.

### The current workflow (what they do now — the guesswork loop)

1. Scan tags
2. Ask agents what they see a lot
3. Look at the loudest complaint
4. Check a few macros
5. Write a doc from internal assumptions
6. Hope customers can find it

The loop: Customer can't find answer → opens ticket → support answers manually
→ answer disappears into ticket history → another customer asks same thing
next week.

## SECTION 2 — Mechanism / Solution Reveal (how it works, what's different)

### The core mechanism (one paragraph)

Support Ticket Deflection turns your support tickets into a ranked fix list
for your help center. Upload a CSV of past tickets. Within 24 hours, you get:

1. **The questions your customers keep asking** — in their own words, ranked
   by how often they reach support.
2. **The customer-term-to-doc-term gaps** — the mismatches that keep people
   from finding answers you've already written.
3. **The source tickets and quotes** behind each recommendation.
4. **A drafted FAQ for each gap.**

You review and publish. Nothing goes live without you.

### What makes it different (contrast with current workflow)

| Current way | This way |
|---|---|
| Scan tags, poll agents, chase complaints | CSV upload → ranked fix list in 24 hours |
| Write docs from internal assumptions | Write docs from customer language extracted from real tickets |
| Guess which fixes matter most | Frequency-ranked, not opinion-ranked |
| Hope customers find it | Customer language matches Google searches → Google deflects |
| No feedback loop | Every recommendation links to source tickets |
| "The bot just sent the wrong article" | You review and publish. Nothing goes live without you. |

### The SEO mechanism (the multiplier)

- Pages written in the language people search get more visibility on Google.
  This is the foundational principle of search — not a theory, not a case
  study required.
- Your ticket queue already contains that language. Customers type "how do I
  cancel my account." Your help center says "Subscription Termination
  Procedure." Google can't match them. Close the gap, and Google starts
  sending customers to your page instead of your support form.
- The mechanism is proven — demonstrable by any buyer in thirty seconds with
  their own Google search.

### What it does NOT do (boundaries — builds trust)

- Does not answer tickets.
- Does not touch your help desk.
- Does not publish anything without your review.
- Does not replace agents.
- Does not require an implementation consultant or six-month rollout.

### The output (what they actually receive)

- Repeat question clusters with frequency ranking
- Customer wording (verbatim, from tickets)
- Term mappings (customer language → doc language → gap)
- Source ticket IDs for every recommendation
- Evidence quotes from real tickets
- Drafted FAQs, ready to review/edit/publish

1. **Source-ticket traceability.** Every recommendation links back to the
   specific tickets and verbatim customer quotes that produced it. Nothing

2. **Frequency-ranked, not opinion-ranked.** The fix list is ordered by how
   often each question reaches support — not by which agent complained

## SECTION 4 — Objection Handling (you tried X, here's why it underperformed)

### Objection 1: "We hired more agents."

**Why it didn't work:** 30–45% annual turnover. 69–73% first-year attrition.
$10K–$20K per replacement. Linear hiring is mathematically losing — you're
filling a leaky bucket.

**What's different:** Support Ticket Deflection removes repeat questions from
the queue. Your existing agents do the work they were hired for.

### Objection 2: "We built out our FAQ / knowledge base."

**Why it didn't work:** Most KB projects degrade within 6 months. No one owns
the ticket → article feedback loop. 43% of self-service failures happen
because customers can't find relevant content — even when it exists (Gartner
2024). Content goes stale. Search is poor.

**What's different:** Every recommendation is sourced from a real ticket where
a customer couldn't find the answer. You're filling the exact gaps your queue
already identified — not guessing what to write.

### Objection 3: "We deployed a chatbot / AI agent."

**Why it didn't work:** Klarna's CEO publicly admitted the AI-only strategy
produced "lower quality." Intercom Fin's real-world resolution rates sit at
42–50% in vendor case studies; community threads report "12% real resolve
rate" because conversations are marked "assumed resolved" when agents step in.
Per-resolution pricing creates budget volatility. Performance degrades as
content goes stale.

**What's different:** Support Ticket Deflection doesn't answer tickets. It
doesn't touch your help desk. The AI risk is zero because the AI never talks
to a customer. It reads your queue, finds the pattern, hands you a publishing
plan.

### Objection 4: "We wrote macros / canned responses."

**Why it didn't work:** Macros go outdated as product changes. They're the
primary source of "the bot just sent the wrong article" issues. Gorgias's own
product copy: "Generic, robotic responses frustrate customers."

**What's different:** FAQs are drafted from actual agent language that resolved
real tickets. The language is already proven because it already worked.

### Objection 5: "We outsourced to a BPO."

**Why it didn't work:** First-year attrition runs higher offshore. Quality
declines on complex tickets. Klarna's pivot — BPO → AI → back to humans — is
the canonical case study.

**What's different:** You keep control. You review. You publish. Nothing goes
live without you.

### Objection 6: "We don't know which tickets to fix first."

**Why it didn't work:** The current method — scanning tags, polling agents,
chasing the loudest complaint — guesses at what to fix. Most support leads
can name their top 3 repeat questions. They can't name the top 10, the exact
customer phrasing, the doc gaps causing search failure, or which fix would
deflect the most tickets next month.

**What's different:** The fix list is frequency-ranked from your own queue.
The queue sets the priority. You're not guessing.

### Objection 7: "Our help center already has the answers."

**Why it doesn't deflect:** 73% of customers attempt self-service. Only 14%
resolve there (Gartner 2024). The answers exist — they're just written in
internal language customers don't search. Your doc says "Subscription
Termination." Your customer searched "how do I cancel." Google couldn't match
them. The page exists. It's invisible.

**What's different:** Support Ticket Deflection surfaces every mismatch between
what customers search and what your docs say — ranked by frequency. You close
the gap. Google starts deflecting.
