# Body Copy — Section 1: Problem Agitation

---

Gartner benchmarked the cost of answering the same question twice. A
self-service resolution costs $1.84. A human-handled contact costs $13.50.
That is a 7.3x multiplier on every ticket that should never have reached an
agent.

HDI's 2024 Support Center Practices report puts the gap wider: $22.00 for a
live agent interaction versus $2.93 for self-service.

Across the full customer journey — search, fail, switch channels, repeat
information, wait — Gartner found the gap reaches 80 to 100 times the cost of
a fully self-served resolution.

And 40% to 60% of your inbound volume is repetitive. Password resets. "Where
is my order." "How do I cancel." "Why was I charged." Questions your help
center was built to answer. Questions your team answers again, and again, and
again.

---

A customer cannot find the answer in your help center. They open a ticket.
Your team replies — fast, competent, within the hour. The answer exists now,
inside the ticket thread.

Then it disappears back into the queue.

Next week, a different customer asks the same question. The answer has to be
written again, from scratch, by someone who already wrote it the week before.

Gartner found that 74% of customer issues that reach a live agent could have
been resolved through self-service — if the relevant knowledge article existed
or was findable.

The current method for figuring out which articles to write is mostly
guesswork. Scan the tags. Ask agents what they see a lot. Look at the loudest
complaint. Check a few macros. Write a doc from internal assumptions. Hope
customers can find it.

Most support leads can name their top three repeat questions. What they cannot
name: the top ten, the exact words customers use, the specific doc gaps that
cause the search failure, and which fix would deflect the most tickets next
month.

---

Salesforce surveyed 5,500 service professionals across 30 countries. The
finding: agents spend 39% of their time actually servicing customers. The
remaining 61% goes to internal meetings, administrative tasks, manually logging
case notes, and toggling between systems to pull customer history.

Research from Gorgias found that support agents lose five hours a week to
repetitive tickets — copy-pasting order statuses, return policies, answers
they have already written hundreds of times.

Industry analysis of support workflows indicates that for every 90 seconds an
agent spends typing a reply, they spend up to 10 minutes assembling context —
toggling between the ticketing tool, the CRM, the billing platform, pulling
the customer's history, verifying the account state.

53% of agents at underperforming organizations toggle between multiple screens
to find what they need. At high performers, that number drops to 36%.

NICE's research on contact center burnout captured what agents say about this
work: "Answering the same question over and over again isn't very rewarding and
handling back-to-back customer complaints all day takes its toll." "A day in
the life of a contact center agent is often filled with mind-numbing
repetition."

---

Annual contact center attrition sits at 31% to 45%, depending on the sector.
High-stress environments reach 55% to 61%. First-year attrition alone runs
69% to 73%.

Replacing a single agent costs $10,000 to $20,000 in direct recruiting,
onboarding, and training expenses. When you factor in lost productivity
during the six-to-eight-month ramp to full performance, the customer
experience degradation while a new hire learns the product, the morale tax
on the remaining team covering the vacancy, and the institutional knowledge
that walked out the door — the total cumulative impact of losing one agent
reaches $22,500 to $46,000.

For a 100-agent support center at the industry-average turnover rate, that is
$2.25 million to $4.6 million a year on attrition management alone.

90% of IT support professionals say repetitive manual tasks actively
contribute to low team morale. 74% experience ongoing burnout. 47% of call
center managers cite high turnover and absenteeism as their single biggest
operating problem.

Insignia Resources, whose benchmarks are cited across the contact center
industry, calls this the "repetitive ticket trap": repetition drives burnout,
burnout drives turnover, turnover drives cost, and the remaining agents absorb
more repetition.

---

Gartner surveyed 5,728 customers. The finding: 73% of customers attempt
self-service at some point during their support journey. Only 14% fully
resolve there.

The other 59% arrive at your queue already frustrated. 43% say they could not
find content relevant to their issue — even when it existed. 45% say the
company did not understand what they were trying to do.

Zendesk's 2026 CX Trends report, based on 11,000 respondents across 22
countries, found that 74% of customers are frustrated when they have to repeat
information. 85% of CX leaders say customers will drop brands over unresolved
issues — even on the first contact.

The CEB research behind *The Effortless Experience* — analysis of 97,000
customers — is the canonical study on what this frustration costs: 94% of
customers with low-effort interactions intend to repurchase. Only 4% of
high-effort customers report the same intent. 81% of high-effort customers
intend to spread negative word of mouth.

Nick Toman, co-author of the study, described the measurement gap: "We count
that as yet another 'first contact resolved,' but the customer views it as 'I
had to contact you twice.'"

Forrester's 2024 US Customer Experience Index — 98,000 customers, 223 brands —
found the average CX score at an all-time low of 69.3. The third consecutive
year of decline. Rick Parrish, Forrester's VP and Research Director: "US
consumers are having, on average, the worst experiences in a decade."

In 2025, the same index found that 25% of US brands' CX rankings declined.
Only 7% improved.

---

Service leaders themselves report that 20% to 40% of their current live volume
could be resolved in self-service channels. They know the volume is there.

But 56% of leaders are adding new channels rather than fixing the ones they
already have.

81% of consumers want more self-service options. Leaders estimate that demand
at around 60% — a 21-point perception gap that leads to chronic
underinvestment.

61% of service teams believe they are proactive in addressing customer issues.
Only 33% of customers agree. A 28-point gap between what the team thinks it is
doing and what the customer actually experiences.

Businesses underestimate the number of poor customer experiences they deliver
by an average of 38%.

The problem is not that leaders do not know repeat tickets are an issue. It is
that they do not know the shape of it — which questions cost the most, which
fixes would deflect the most volume, and what exact words their customers are
using when they search, fail, and open a ticket instead.

The data to answer those questions already exists. It is in the ticket queue.
The next section explains how to extract it.
