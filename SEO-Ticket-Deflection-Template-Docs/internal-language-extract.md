# Internal Employee Language Extract — Support Ticket Deflection

Every phrase below is how support leaders, agents, and industry insiders
actually talk about the repeat-ticket problem. Extracted from three research
briefs (2026-05-27). Organized by who says it, not by what we want them to say.

---

## 1. SUPPORT LEADERS (VPs, Directors, Heads of Support)

### How they describe the problem to peers

- "We keep answering the same five questions fifty times a day."
  — Support Driven community, verbatim phrasing used to admit defeat to peers.
  **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, flagged as anchor phrase.

- "Our top-10 tickets haven't changed in a year."
  — The phrasing support leaders use to admit the problem is structural, not
  seasonal. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Support Driven community.

- "The bot is basically a filter before they reach a human."
  — Gergely Orosz (The Pragmatic Engineer) on Klarna's AI. Used by leaders to
  puncture overconfidence in existing chatbots. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook.

- "What percentage of your tier-1 queue is the same five questions?"
  — Discovery question support leaders ask each other. **Source:**
  Repeat-Question Playbook.

- "How many of your tickets are WISMO or ping-pong before an agent can
  actually answer?"
  — Insider diagnostic question. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook.

- "What's your containment rate net of conversations your AI marked 'assumed
  resolved'?"
  — The honest conversation support leaders have after AI deployments.
  **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Stage 3 discovery call script.

- "If you ran the math — annual ticket volume × % repeat questions × your
  assisted cost per contact — what number do you get?"
  — The back-of-the-envelope diagnostic that anchors sales conversations.
  **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Stage 3 discovery call script.

### How they describe what they've already tried

- "I assume you've already tried scaling headcount. The unit economics on
  tier-1 attrition probably told you that wasn't working."
  — Acknowledgment of the hiring trap. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook,
  Angle 4.

- "Most KB projects degrade within 6 months because nobody owns the feedback
  loop from ticket → article gap."
  — Acknowledgment of the documentation maintenance problem. **Source:**
  Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

- "Everyone has a chatbot story now. The Klarna walk-back made it
  conversationally acceptable to admit it."
  — Acknowledgment of AI deployment disappointment. **Source:**
  Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

- "Most teams I talk to have AI in production. The honest conversation isn't
  'do you have AI,' it's 'what's your real resolution rate after you back out
  the assumed-resolved conversations?'"
  — The post-AI-deployment reality check. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook, Angle 4.

### How they describe the metrics problem

- "If 20% of your queue is the same five questions, your blended CPT is hiding
  a Tier-1 cost problem and a Tier-2 underinvestment at the same time."
  — How support leaders explain the cost-per-ticket blindspot. **Source:**
  Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 2.

- "Most teams report 75% FCR. Dixon's research says the customer-reported
  number is closer to 40%. What does your repeat-contact rate look like?"
  — How support leaders challenge their own FCR numbers. **Source:**
  Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 2.

- "What percentage of your tickets are about something you've already
  documented?"
  — The discovery question almost no prospect can answer. **Source:**
  Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 2, FLAGGED metric.

### How they describe the leadership blindspot

- Service leaders themselves report that "as much as 40% of today's live
  volume could be resolved in self-service channels." — Gartner / Devin
  Poole, "Delivering on the Digital Promise," 2019.

- 56% of service leaders are "adding new channels rather than fixing existing
  ones." — Same source.

- Leaders estimate self-service demand at ~60%. Actual consumer demand: 81%.
  21-point perception gap. — EasyVista / CX Network.

- "Businesses underestimate the number of times customers have poor
  experiences by an average of 38%." — Khoros / Forrester Consulting, 2024.

- "61% of service teams believe they are proactive in addressing issues —
  only 33% of customers agree." 28-point self-perception gap. — Salesforce,
  State of Service 6th Edition.

## 2. FRONTLINE AGENTS (how they describe their daily experience)

### Verbatim descriptions of the work

- "Answering the same question over and over again isn't very rewarding and
  handling back-to-back customer complaints all day takes its toll."
  — NICE corporate blog, "5 Ways to Prevent Contact Center Agent Burnout."
  **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 1.

- "A day in the life of a contact center agent is often filled with
  mind-numbing repetition... Just because someone is good at a particular
  task doesn't mean they want to do it forever."
  — NICE, same source. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 1.

- "Three out of four respondents said agents are overwhelmed by too many
  systems and too much information, causing longer calls and weaker outcomes."
  — Deloitte 2024 Global Contact Center Survey. **Source:** Cost brief, §4.

- "60% of agents report that their training provides no value."
  — Deloitte 2024 Global Contact Center Survey. **Source:** Cost brief, §4.

- "Over half [of agents] say they've experienced burnout, whether they work
  in a contact center or in the field."
  — Salesforce, State of Service 6th Edition, April 2024. **Source:** Cost
  brief, §4.

- "53% of agents at underperforming organizations toggle between multiple
  screens to find what they need — compared to 36% at high performers."
  — Salesforce, State of Service 6th Edition. **Source:** Cost brief, §2.

- "77% of agents report increased and more complex workloads compared to a
  year prior."
  — Salesforce, State of Service 6th Edition. **Source:** Cost brief, §2.

### How they describe the context-switching tax

- The true waste is "not just the 90 seconds spent typing a canned macro
  reply, but the surrounding 10-minute context-switching tax spent toggling
  between ticketing tools, CRMs, and billing platforms to pull historical
  customer states."
  — Industry synthesis, Friction Multiplier §2. **Source:** Friction
  Multiplier.

- "Generic, robotic responses frustrate customers."
  — Gorgias product copy on why macros fail. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook, Angle 4.

### How they describe the self-service sabotage

- "60% of customer service agents fail to promote self-service options...
  when agents do mention self-service in customer interactions, 25% make
  neutral comments, and 12% make explicitly negative remarks."
  — Gartner, June 2, 2025 press release (Keith McIntosh). **Source:** Cost
  brief, §6.

- Agent endorsement of self-service is associated with "a doubling of
  customer adoption next time." — Same source.

### How they describe the AI reality

- "12% real resolve rate while being charged for significantly more" —
  support managers in community threads on Intercom Fin, because conversations
  were marked "assumed resolved" when agents stepped in. **Source:**
  Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

- "The bot just sent the wrong article" — primary macro failure mode.
  **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

## 3. INDUSTRY ANALYSTS & RESEARCHERS (canonical quotes)

### On the repetition problem

- Eric Keller, Senior Director, Gartner: "No one wakes up in the morning
  saying, 'Hey, I'm really looking forward to calling customer service today.'
  But an even worse scenario is spending 30 minutes online trying to get
  something done, then that not working and having to call in anyway."
  — CX Dive, August 2024. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

- Eric Keller, Gartner: "While 73% of customers use self-service at some
  point in their customer service journey, it's concerning to see that so few
  fully resolve there."
  — Gartner press release, August 19, 2024. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook, Angle 6.

### On rep burnout and the human cost

- Devin Poole, Senior Director Analyst, Gartner: "An increase in assisted
  channel contact volumes has wide-ranging impact on the service function —
  declines in customer experience, higher cost-to-serve and rep burnout."
  — Gartner press release, March 10, 2021. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook, Angle 6.

- Devin Poole, Gartner: "While there will always be live service, that type
  of service should be treated like a precious resource and reserved for
  opportunities that significantly move the dial on outcomes the customers
  and the company care most about."
  — "Delivering on the Digital Promise," 2019. **Source:** Cost brief, §6.

### On the AI walk-back

- Sebastian Siemiatkowski, CEO, Klarna: "As cost unfortunately seems to have
  been a too predominant evaluation factor when organizing this, what you end
  up having is lower quality. Really investing in the quality of the human
  support is the way of the future for us."
  — Bloomberg, May 2025. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

- Klarna spokesperson: "Some customers get an amazing agent, some a less
  engaged agent, prompting repeated contacts and higher costs."
  — SiliconRepublic, May 2025. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

### On FCR being a lie

- Matthew Dixon, co-author The Effortless Experience: "The best low effort
  companies don't just focus on solving the issue that you call in about.
  They're thinking one step ahead for the customer and resolving the issue
  the customer might call back about."
  — CEO Coaching International interview. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook, Angle 6.

- Nick Toman, co-author The Effortless Experience: "We count that as yet
  another 'first contact resolved,' but the customer views it as 'I had to
  contact you twice.'"
  — Help Scout HelpU. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

### On agent enablement and documentation

- Mat Patterson, Customer Service Educator, Help Scout: "Do they understand
  the value of a great knowledge base as a tool to reduce effort for customers
  and make their own jobs less repetitive? If not, begin the discussion
  there."
  — Help Scout blog. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

- Sarah Hatter, Founder of CoSupport and ElevateCX: "It is your fault because
  the documentation of the training wasn't clear. They didn't understand if
  they don't understand how to use your product. That's your fault for
  training and onboarding."
  — The James Nathan Experience podcast, S4E1. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook, Angle 6.

### On the AI reality check

- Kate Leggett, VP and Principal Analyst, Forrester: "While the vision of
  AI-first customer service is compelling — autonomous operations, specialized
  human agents, and seamless self-service — most organizations are not yet
  equipped to deliver it."
  — Forrester, November 10, 2025. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook,
  Angle 6.

- Brad Cleveland, Co-founder and Senior Advisor, ICMI: "There is much hype
  around AI reducing workloads, but we haven't seen that materialize yet. We

  — TechTarget, 2024. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

### On the customer side of repetition

- Shep Hyken: "Customers don't typically notice if they only have to tell

  — Hyken.com. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

### On the CX index decline

- Rick Parrish, VP and Research Director, Forrester: "US consumers are having,

### On automating processes, not people

- Annette Franz, Founder and CEO, CX Journey: "Right now, it's not about

  — Inside Intercom podcast. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 6.

### On the knowledge base model being outdated

- James Millard, VP Global Support and Community, D2L: "The 'traditional'

## 4. INSIDER SHORTHAND (terms that signal you're in the tribe)

| Term | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **WISMO** | "Where Is My Order" — universal shorthand for the highest-volume retail repeat ticket | SocialIntents / Gartner |
| **Ping-pong tickets** | Tickets where agents ask 3+ follow-up questions before they can even start helping | SocialIntents, citing Gartner |
| **Tier-1 fatigue** | Burnout caused by answering the same low-complexity questions repeatedly | NICE |
| **Documentation debt** | "The number of support tickets that could have been prevented with better documentation" — tracked metric | Ariglad |
| **Knowledge gap** | Intercom Fin's dashboard term for missing/outdated help content that causes deflection failure | Intercom |
| **Self-service abandonment** | When customers start in self-service but give up and open a ticket | Gartner / Devin Poole |
| **Next issue avoidance** | Addressing adjacent downstream issues in the first contact to prevent a callback | The Effortless Experience (Dixon, Toman, DeLisi) |
| **Repetitive ticket trap** | The cycle where repetition → burnout → turnover → more repetition for remaining agents | Insignia Resources |
| **CRM-context tax** | The 10 minutes of toggling between systems for every 90 seconds of actual reply typing | Industry synthesis, Friction Multiplier |
| **Assumed resolved** | When an AI marks a conversation resolved because a human stepped in, not because the issue was solved | Intercom Fin user forums |
| **Reactive queue-clearing** | What agents do instead of high-leverage work — just burning down the ticket count | Friction Multiplier §2 |
| **Shift-left** | Moving Tier 1 work to Tier 0 (self-service), Tier 0 work to proactive prevention | Industry standard term |

## 5. WHAT THEY'VE ALREADY TRIED (and how they describe the failure)

### Hiring more agents
- Failure language: "The unit economics on tier-1 attrition probably told you
  that wasn't working." Linear hiring is mathematically losing at 30-45%
  annual turnover + $10K-$20K replacement cost. First-year attrition: 69-73%.
  **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

### Building out FAQ / knowledge base
- Failure language: "Content goes stale, search is poor, nobody owns
  maintenance." Customers raise tickets for issues already documented because
  "they find the content difficult to understand, the search functionality is
  not working correctly, or the knowledge base is not easily accessible"
  (Wowto). 43% of self-service failures: customers can't find relevant content
  (Gartner 2024). **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

### Chatbots
- Failure language: "Everyone has a chatbot story now." Klarna walked back
  AI-only. Air Canada held liable for chatbot errors. British Airways AI gave
  wrong travel advice. Intercom Fin: $0.99/resolution pricing, "12% real
  resolve rate." **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

### Macros / canned responses
- Failure language: "Generic, robotic responses frustrate customers"
  (Gorgias). Macros go outdated as product changes. Primary source of "the bot
  just sent the wrong article" issues. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook,
  Angle 4.

### AI Agents (Fin, Zendesk AI, Ada, Forethought, Einstein)
- Failure language: Per-resolution pricing creates budget volatility.
  "Resolution" definitions are gameable. Performance degrades as content goes
  stale. "The honest conversation isn't 'do you have AI,' it's 'what's your
  real resolution rate after you back out the assumed-resolved conversations?'"
  **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

### Offshore / BPO
- Failure language: First-year attrition runs higher offshore. Quality decline
  on complex tickets. Klarna's pivot away from BPO → AI → back to humans is
  the canonical case study. **Source:** Repeat-Question Playbook, Angle 4.

### Self-service portals / community forums
- Failure language: Higher Logic: ~1 in 5 pageviews on accepted-answer threads
  results in deflection; ~1 in 20 on general KB articles. Most community
  engagement is "curiosity," not deflection. **Source:** Repeat-Question
  Playbook, Angle 4.

## 6. CUSTOMER-SIDE LANGUAGE (what customers say when self-service fails)

- "The company didn't understand what they were trying to do."
  — 45% of customers who started in self-service. Gartner, August 2024
  (n=5,728). **Source:** Cost brief, §5.

- "I couldn't find content relevant to my issue."
  — 43% of self-service failures. Gartner, August 2024. **Source:** Cost
  brief, §5.

- "74% are frustrated when they have to repeat information."
  — Zendesk CX Trends 2026 (n=11,000+). **Source:** Cost brief, §5.

- "85% of CX leaders say customers will drop brands over unresolved issues,
  even on the first contact."
  — Zendesk CX Trends 2026. **Source:** Cost brief, §5.

- "32% of customers said that, after just one negative experience, new and
  current customers would stop doing business with a brand or company they'd
  previously loved."
  — PwC, Future of Customer Experience Survey. **Source:** Cost brief, §5.

- "More than 58% of consumers are unable to resolve their issues on the web,
  despite their best efforts."
  — Nuance Communications, December 2013. **Source:** Cost brief, §5.

- "59% of consumers are frustrated that they have to reach out to a live agent
  in customer service to resolve their issue."
  — Nuance Communications, December 2013. **Source:** Cost brief, §5.

- **For landing-page copy:** Pull from §1 (leader language) and §3 (analyst
  quotes). These are the phrases that make a support lead think "they
  understand my world."
- **For ad copy / subject lines:** Pull from §4 (insider shorthand). "WISMO,"
  "documentation debt," "ping-pong tickets" — these signal competence in 3
  words.

  the failure before pitching the fix.

  These are the phrases that make a support lead feel seen.
