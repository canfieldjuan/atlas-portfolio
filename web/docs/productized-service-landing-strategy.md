# Productized Service Landing Page Strategy — Demo-First, Outcome-Led

Last updated: May 2, 2026.

Internal-only. Captures the conversion logic shared across productized service landing pages where the buyer is an **outcome-preference buyer** (not a B2B systems buyer). The whole approach diverges from the AI Content Ops Station landing because the buyer mental model is fundamentally different — outcome buyers want to see the deliverable, not the machinery.

## Implemented pages

| Page | Niche | Trial | Monthly ladder |
|---|---|---|---|
| `/podcast-repurposing` | Business / consultant / founder-led podcast hosts | $149 one-time | $497 / $997 / $1,997 |
| `/dark-story-engine` | Dark / mystery YouTube narration channels (horror, urban legends, paranormal, cryptids) | $99 one-time | $497 / $997 / $1,997 |

Both pages share the same monthly ladder ($497 / $997 / $1,997). Trial floors differ because per-package value differs — a podcast episode delivers six multi-channel assets, a YouTube video package delivers six single-video production assets. The script-only nature of YouTube delivery anchors the trial $50 lower.

## Core principle

The buyer can understand the offer in one sentence:

- **Podcast:** "I give you one episode. You give me multiple usable content assets."
- **Dark Story Engine:** "I give you a topic. You give me a full upload-ready video package."

The page does not need to educate them for 3,000 words. The demo carries the sale.

## Niche selection

Pick **ONE narrow lane** per page. Niche-pickers (where the buyer self-selects from multiple verticals) are deferred until there are multiple verticals live. For a single vertical, one clear lane converts better — buyers do not have to classify themselves before they feel the offer.

The niche should be tight enough that the headline can name the buyer specifically:

- "Built for consultants, founders, and operators running business podcasts."
- "Built for dark story narration, urban-legend, paranormal, and dark-mystery YouTube channels."

The buyer profile should be specific about willingness to pay. Don't price hobby tier; price professional tier. Both implemented pages target buyers who use their channel as an authority / lead-gen / revenue surface, not enthusiasts.

## Page shape

Short above-the-fold + strong sample-output demo + modular sections below. Not a long-form VSL.

The first version of any productized-service page should answer only four questions:

1. **What do I give you?** (input)
2. **What do I get back?** (output)
3. **Will it sound/feel like me?** (voice / quality)
4. **How much does it cost?** (pricing)

Everything else is supporting evidence.

## Section order (in priority)

### 1. Above the fold

- **Headline** that names the outcome, not the system.
  - Podcast: "Turn one business podcast episode into a week of authority content."
  - Dark Story Engine: "Ship a dark story video every week — without writing the script yourself."
- **Subhead** that names inputs and outputs concretely. Lists the actual deliverables ("newsletter, blog post, LinkedIn post, X thread…" / "narration script, title variants, thumbnail brief…").
- **Primary CTA** that names the action a buyer would describe to a friend. ("Try one episode for $149." / "Try one video package for $99.") Better than "Get started."
- **Secondary CTA** that scrolls to the demo, not to pricing. The demo is the sale.

### 2. Sample-outputs demo (the sales engine)

This is the most important block on the page. Place it immediately below the fold.

- Show 2–3 sample inputs / niches. Buyer picks the one closest to their work.
- For each sample, render the full output package as visible artifacts: every output type as a separate tab, with real-feeling content.
- No pipeline visualization. No "stages." No reasoning-layer talk. Just deliverables, side by side, with the source input at the top so the buyer remembers what went in.
- Demo replaces the "feature list." A buyer who can SEE the deliverables does not need a paragraph describing them.

The demo's job: make the buyer think *"damn, I could use that."*

### 3. Pain section

Short. Two or three paragraphs. Names the recurring failure mode in their language.

- Podcast: "You record the episode, post the link once, and the best ideas die there."
- Dark Story Engine: "The script is what is breaking your cadence. Not the recording. The blank page on Sunday night."

Do not over-explain. The pain is obvious to the buyer; the page just needs to say it back to them.

### 4. Deliverables section

**Six core outputs is the canonical package size.** Keeps cognitive load low (buyers can hold six items in their head) and matches what Authority-tier scope can comfortably deliver weekly without burning out production.

The exact six vary per niche:

| Podcast Repurposing | Dark Story Engine |
|---|---|
| Email newsletter | Full narration script |
| SEO blog post | Title variants (CTR-optimized) |
| LinkedIn post | Thumbnail concept brief |
| X / Twitter thread | Description + timestamps + tags |
| Short-form video script | Pinned engagement comment |
| Pull quotes + promo captions | Shorts / TikTok teaser |

The page also lists secondary deliverables under "every [unit] CAN become…" framing — phrasing matters because it lets tier packages flex without overpromising every asset in every tier. The demo renders all six core outputs as tabs the buyer can scan side-by-side.

**Upsells should NOT be more random asset types.** Upsells happen around **strategy, volume, and publishing support**: voice profile, content calendar, SEO keyword targeting, story-sourcing help, thumbnail revisions, monthly performance review, publishing/scheduling support.

### 5. Voice / quality section

Reframes "quality gates" / "approval queue" / system language into buyer language:

- "Built from the [episode/topic], not guessed from a prompt."
- "Your tone, not GPT's."
- "Voice-matched before delivery."
- "Calibrated to your archive."

Buyers do not want to hear about the QA pipeline. They want to know it will sound like them.

### 6. How it works

Four steps maximum, in buyer language. No pipeline diagrams.

Podcast version: paste your episode link → we pull out the strongest ideas → you get publish-ready assets → review, edit, publish.

Dark Story Engine version: send the topic → we draft the full package → you record and upload → we learn the channel.

### 7. Pricing

#### Principles

- Lead with the **trial** (low-friction paid sample).
- Then **2–3 monthly tiers**, with the middle tier visually highlighted as "MOST POPULAR." Do not highlight the most expensive plan — that reads pushy.
- Three monthly tiers is justified IF every tier sits above the "decision paralysis at $297" floor. Below that price, the third tier becomes friction without revenue — drop it.
- Position **between DIY tools and full-service agencies.** Don't compete with cheap software. Price as the leveraged-service alternative to hiring an agency.

#### The $497 / $997 / $1,997 monthly ladder

Both implemented pages use this ladder. Same spread (2× per step) gives the buyer a memorable mental model for stepping up. The trial floor is the only per-page variable:

| Tier | Podcast Repurposing | Dark Story Engine |
|---|---|---|
| Trial | $149 (1 episode, 6 assets) | $99 (1 video package, 6 outputs) |
| Entry | Starter $497/mo | Channel Cadence $497/mo |
| Main offer (MOST POPULAR) | Growth $997/mo | Channel Engine $997/mo |
| Ceiling | Authority $1,997/mo | Channel Authority $1,997/mo |

The page should sell the **trial hard**, then convert satisfied buyers into the $997/month plan. That's the funnel.

### 8. FAQ

5–7 buyer-blocking questions, not generic ones. Pre-bake the questions that filter out bad-fit leads. Examples that worked:

- Voice match mechanics
- Cancellation / pause terms
- What counts as in-scope vs. project work
- Hard niche exclusions (e.g., Dark Story Engine explicitly excludes true crime — the FAQ flags this and routes those buyers elsewhere before they pay)

Each FAQ entry is also emitted as `FAQPage` JSON-LD via `generateFaqJsonLd` from `@/lib/seo` for SEO surface.

### 9. Final CTA

Mirror the trial offer, not subscription. "Give us one episode" / "Send us one topic" is lower commitment and easier to say yes to than "subscribe."

## Checkout flow

- Read selected plan from the URL (`?plan=trial|...`). Server-side validate against a known set; bogus values fall back to trial.
- Two-step layout: (1) tell us about your [show/channel], (2) payment.
- Step 1: 5 fields max — name, email, [show/channel] name, [episode/channel] link, optional [voice/story] notes.
- Step 2: payment block visually present but **stubbed** until Stripe wires up. Honest copy: "Card processing is launching soon. We email you a secure payment link within one business day."
- Sticky order-summary sidebar (`lg:sticky lg:top-32`) so the plan stays visible through the form scroll.
- Submit posts through the existing `/api/audit` endpoint with the form data mapped to the audit payload shape. **No backend changes.** Payment integration is the only follow-up.
- Server-side validates the `?plan=` param so the right plan info ships in the initial HTML — no flash of wrong plan after hydration.

## Central positioning bridges

Use these as the bridge sentence when articulating to anyone WHY each offer exists:

- **Podcast:** "Your podcast is not one piece of content. It is the raw material for your entire content engine."
- **Dark Story Engine:** "The script is what is breaking your cadence. Not the recording, not the editing — the blank page on Sunday night. We solve that."

These connect back to the AI Content Ops engine without making the buyer care about the machinery. They are buying leverage on what they already do, not buying access to a system.

## What NOT to do

- **Don't lead with "AI" or "AI Content Ops" in buyer-facing copy.** The product name can stay in the eyebrow; the system name belongs internal.
- **Don't show the pipeline diagram on this page.** It is the wrong mental model for this buyer.
- **Don't pad the page with feature comparisons against generic AI writers.** Buyers in this segment are comparing to "doing it themselves" or "hiring an agency," not to other AI tools.
- **Don't use the audit-pilot-build commercial structure** that works for B2B systems buyers. That model presumes the buyer wants diagnostics first. Productized-service buyers want to see the deliverable and pick a tier.
- **Don't pre-load too many demo samples.** 2–3 archetypes is plenty. More dilutes the "this is built for me" feeling.

## Future candidates

The same structure applies to:

- **Recruiter / employer brand content** — input is careers page + Glassdoor handle, output is recruiting collateral
- **Real estate listing content** — input is MLS / Zillow URL, output is listing copy + market report + agent newsletter
- **E-commerce product descriptions** — input is product URL, output is improved description + FAQ + comparison content
- **Newsletter operators** — input is RSS feed, output is curated newsletter draft

For each, the four buyer questions stay the same. Only the inputs, outputs, and demo samples change.
