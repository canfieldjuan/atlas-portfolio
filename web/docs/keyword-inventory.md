## How to use this for ads

Each cluster below maps roughly to a **landing page** + **buyer intent**. When building
a Google Ads campaign:

- One **ad group per cluster** is a reasonable starting structure (don't bid on all
  116 in one ad group — Quality Score will tank).
- Use the cluster's primary terms as **exact / phrase match** in the ad group, and
  the broader sitewide terms as background/expansion.
- The page paths shown next to each keyword tell you **where to send the click**.
- Keywords with `(sitewide)` are the default fallback set; use them sparingly in ads
  (broad and competitive — better as remarketing or brand-defense).

## Gaps worth considering for ads (NOT currently in metadata)

These are deliberately absent today; mention them as bid candidates worth testing:

- **Geography:** "AI consultant Chicago", "AI automation consultant US", "AI consultant remote US" — geo-overlay terms keep CPC low and reach focused.
- **Industry verticals:** "AI for SaaS companies", "AI automation for B2B SaaS",
  "AI for ecommerce ops", "AI for marketing operations", "AI for customer success".
- **Buyer roles:** "AI consultant for CTO", "AI automation for Head of Operations",
  "AI consultant for VP Marketing", "AI for Heads of CS".
- **Outcome / pain phrasing:** "fix manual content workflow", "stop writing weekly
  blog posts", "AI without writing custom code", "AI consultant fixed price".
- **Competitor / alternative terms:** "AI consultant alternatives to [agency]",
  "alternative to Gainsight" (for the B2B churn product when that ships).

These shouldn't go into page-metadata `keywords` arrays (HTML keyword meta is mostly
ignored by Google now anyway), but they're useful as ad-bid candidates and as
content prompts if the resource library expands.
