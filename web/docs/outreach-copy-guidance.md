# Outreach Copy Guidance

This document is the repo-safe playbook for Support Ticket Deflection outreach.
The generated Apollo CSVs are working files and should stay outside source
control. Use this doc to keep copy, assets, and follow-up strategy consistent as
the prospect lists grow.

## Core Rule

Sell the problem differently by role.

- Business buyers need the cost and urgency case.
- Support leaders need proof that the queue is repeating itself.
- Operators need relief from repeated work.
- Champions need a forwardable observation.
- Technical evaluators need risk controls.
- Partners need a diagnostic they can use with clients.

Short copy opens the door. Longer copy closes doubt.

## Category Copy Lanes

| Outreach category | Copy lane | What resonates | What moves them to act | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `economic_buyer_wallet_owner` | Cost leak and founder margin | Payroll waste, margin pressure, churn risk, fast proof, no new platform rollout | Make repeat tickets feel like a measurable operating cost they can inspect this week | Deep workflow detail, content-strategy language, chatbot framing, hard deflection percentage promises |
| `department_buyer_budget_recommender` | Support leader pain and proof | Recurring tickets, self-service failure, customer wording gaps, evidence-backed prioritization, review-ready drafts | Show a cleaner way to turn known support pain into a ranked publishing plan their team can trust | Replacing agents, pretending every ticket can be deflected, guaranteed queue reduction |
| `operator_manager_workflow_owner` | Team relief and workflow cleanup | Agent time, repeated manual answers, QA consistency, cleaner handoff from tickets to docs | Promise less copy-paste work and a practical list of what to publish first | Executive ROI as the opener, abstract SEO language, anything that sounds like more admin work |
| `frontline_operator_champion` | Forwardable observation | Daily frustration, repeated customer phrasing, obvious doc misses, easy internal forwarding | Give them language that names the pain they already see and makes them useful when they pass it up | Asking them to buy, approve budget, or own the project |
| `technical_product_evaluator` | Risk controls and deterministic output | CSV-in/report-out, deterministic analysis, no auto-publish, no customer-facing bot, verifiable source quotes | Lower perceived risk by making the system inspectable, bounded, and easy to reject if the data is not there | Vague AI claims, black-box language, implying it touches production systems |
| `partner_agency_leader_channel_gate` | Partner diagnostic and client expansion | Better discovery, stronger client recommendations, Zendesk/help-center cleanup, packaged advisory work | Frame it as a diagnostic they can use to uncover client ticket-volume opportunities without replacing their services | Bypassing the partner, competing with implementation services, generic SaaS resale copy |
| `partner_delivery_practitioner` | Delivery shortcut and client proof | Faster discovery, fewer opinion debates, ticket-backed recommendations, clearer content backlog | Give practitioners evidence they can take into client delivery conversations | Buyer-level margin pressure or channel economics as the opener |
| `ambiguous_needs_enrichment` | Light discovery | Clear ownership question, low-pressure routing, finding the support/docs owner | Make it easy to redirect to the person who owns support tickets, docs, or customer education | Heavy claims, pricing, assuming budget authority |

## Asset Routing

Use calculators only where they help the buyer make or justify a decision. Do
not send them to every contact.

| Outreach category | Recommended asset | Fit | Use it when | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `economic_buyer_wallet_owner` | Executive leaky-bucket calculator | Strong | The contact owns budget or founder-level operating cost | Can I show what this would find in the last 3-6 months of your tickets? |
| `department_buyer_budget_recommender` | Executive leaky-bucket calculator | Strong | The support leader needs internal budget ammunition | Want the calculator and a ticket-backed snapshot that shows which repeat questions are driving the pattern? |
| `operator_manager_workflow_owner` | Simple Support Tax Calculator | Medium | The operator feels repeated work and needs a practical estimate | Want the simple calculator and a ranked list of the repeat questions your team should publish first? |
| `frontline_operator_champion` | Forwardable pain note | Weak calculator fit | The person can identify pain but cannot buy | Is this worth forwarding to whoever owns support docs or ticket volume? |
| `technical_product_evaluator` | Report shape and data flow | Weak calculator fit | Risk, data handling, and determinism matter more than persuasion | Want the report shape, data fields, and deterministic workflow before anyone evaluates ROI? |
| `partner_agency_leader_channel_gate` | Partner client diagnostic calculator | Strong | The partner can use the calculator in discovery or advisory work | Would this be useful as a diagnostic inside your Zendesk or help-center projects? |
| `partner_delivery_practitioner` | Partner delivery discovery note | Medium | The practitioner needs proof and client-discovery context | Would a calculator plus ticket-backed FAQ gap report help during client discovery? |
| `ambiguous_needs_enrichment` | Enrichment first | None | Ownership is unclear | Are you the right person for support ticket volume or help-center improvement? |

## Calculator Guardrails

The calculator estimates cost from the prospect's own inputs. It is not a claim
that the Deflection Report will save a specific amount.

Safe:

- "Use your ticket volume to estimate the visible support-cost leak."
- "Then use the ticket export to find which repeat questions are causing it."
- "This is directional math, not a savings forecast."

Avoid:

- "The report ranks tickets by cost."
- "We guarantee this much savings."
- "This will reduce your queue by X%."
- "The calculator proves the report's ROI."

The Deflection Report ranks by ticket history, repeated-question volume,
customer wording, failure-risk signals, and evidence. The calculator is a
separate persuasion and prioritization asset.

## Copy Length Decision

Use copy length based on awareness, authority, and risk.

| Outreach category | Length | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `economic_buyer_wallet_owner` | Medium to long | They can buy, but need the money case and urgency. |
| `department_buyer_budget_recommender` | Long | They feel the pain and often need internal ammunition. |
| `operator_manager_workflow_owner` | Medium | They understand the pain quickly; show relief, evidence, and next step. |
| `frontline_operator_champion` | Short | They cannot buy; give them a simple forwardable observation. |
| `technical_product_evaluator` | Medium | They need risk controls more than persuasion. |
| `partner_agency_leader_channel_gate` | Medium to long | They need to see how it helps client work and does not compete with them. |
| `partner_delivery_practitioner` | Medium | Practical, project-based explanation beats a full sales letter. |
| `ambiguous_needs_enrichment` | Very short | Do not sell yet. Route to the right owner. |

## Sequence Default

1. Short pain recognition.
2. Proof and mechanism.
3. Asset matched to role.
4. Risk reversal.

Use the long copy in follow-ups, landing pages, partner pages, and internal
forwarding material. Use short copy to earn the first reply or click.
